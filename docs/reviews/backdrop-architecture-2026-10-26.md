# Backdrop architecture review, 2026-10-26

Reviewer: historian. Previews are 200x133, so I judged outlines and building types only.

## thanjavur_a.png: use with a small edit

The vimana is right: a steep pyramid of diminishing tiers under a single dome-shaped capstone, dwarfing everything near it [documented] (RESEARCH 3.1). The low gateway at right is the right size, but a round dome on it would be wrong.

**Edit:** "Change the roof of the small gateway at right to a low barrel-vaulted (wagon-shaped) stone roof; no domes anywhere." The small blue and teal roofs in the foreground also break the VISION.md palette: "repaint the small foreground roofs in umber and thatch tones."

## nagapattinam_c.png: use with a small edit

The ships pass: broad hulls, single masts, square sails [plausible] (3.2). The monastery tower does not. Its tiered pointed spire reads as a later Burmese or Thai stupa. Nineteenth-century witnesses described the "old pagoda" (the Puduveli-gopuram) as a brick tower. Burnell took it for a temple vimana, and Elliot wrote that it had "nothing in common with Hindu or Muhammadan architecture" [documented] (Walter Elliot, *Indian Antiquary* VII, 1878, pp. 224–227, quoted on a forum; I have not seen the drawings). It was also called the "black pagoda" [documented] (same).

**Edit:** "Replace the right-hand tower with a weathered dark-brick square tower of three or four diminishing storeys, each with small arched openings, ending in a flat or rounded stump; no spire, no white or gold."

## kadaram_a.png: use with a small edit

The mountain is defensible as Gunung Jerai. The stilt houses, the boat, and the flat water are right [plausible] (3.3, 3.4). The tall white spire tower at left is not. Bujang Valley shrines (candi) were small brick and laterite buildings on stepped bases [documented] (3.3). My own section 3.3 suggested "one tall tiered brick tower," and that was an invention with no source. I have withdrawn it.

**Edit:** "Replace the tall white spire with a small red-brown brick and laterite shrine on a stepped square base, about two stories high, with a low pyramidal roof; remove the gold from all shrines."
