## 1. How Prince of Persia: The Lost Crown (2024) designs combat, and what reviewers said makes it feel good

### Takeaway
The Lost Crown's combat feel rests on three things. First, a color-coded telegraph language: yellow means parry for a cinematic counter, red means dodge. Second, a parry payoff that is a short, flashy, anime-style cinematic. Third, a meter (Athra) that fills when you land hits and drains when you take damage. Reviewers praised the readable telegraphs and parry spectacle. They found free-form offensive juggling less satisfying than the defensive tests, because most enemies resist juggling and the basic 3-hit string ends in a punishable recovery.

### Cited Findings
- Enemy attack signals are color-coded. Yellow-glowing eyes mean the attack can be parried for a "massive, cinematic counter-attack." Red-glowing eyes mean it cannot be parried and must be dodged. Parrying a basic attack stuns the enemy briefly. — [Ubisoft Montpellier studio post](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/montpellier/news/NvEbHCYiO8TjMNmfchQ6X/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-will-put-your-combat-platforming-and-puzzlesolving-skills-to-the-test)
- Players must "master the timing of his parry and slide mechanics." Combining jump, slide, wall jump, parry, air attacks and directional attacks gives "seemingly endless combos" and makes Sargon feel like "a master swordsman." — [Ubisoft Montpellier](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/montpellier/news/NvEbHCYiO8TjMNmfchQ6X/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-will-put-your-combat-platforming-and-puzzlesolving-skills-to-the-test)
- The Athra gauge fills when Sargon lands hits and drains when he takes damage. It has two levels: level 1 "Super Massive Wave" (heavy damage) and level 2 "Bahman's Breath" (explosion plus healing zone). The abilities are described as "devastating anime-inspired special attacks." — [Ubisoft Montpellier](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/montpellier/news/NvEbHCYiO8TjMNmfchQ6X/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-will-put-your-combat-platforming-and-puzzlesolving-skills-to-the-test)
- Boss design ties attacks to body parts. The manticore boss Jahandar has a venomous tail sting and a horns-down charge that can be parried for large damage. — [Ubisoft Montpellier](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/montpellier/news/NvEbHCYiO8TjMNmfchQ6X/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-will-put-your-combat-platforming-and-puzzlesolving-skills-to-the-test)
- "Skillful play allows you to juggle enemies into the air or drag them across the screen with finesse." However, most enemies resist being juggled or knocked around, and many punish the attempt. — [GamingTrend review](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-review-heir-to-elegance/)
- The basic 3-hit combo ends with a vulnerable recovery, and longer combos are riskier. The reviewer "preferred to fight with structure and efficiency" and found that avoiding heavy commitment helps you weave between enemies. — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-review-heir-to-elegance/)
- "If you carelessly toss out attacks, enemies can eviscerate you in seconds even on the standard difficulty." — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-review-heir-to-elegance/)
- Parries must face the attack's direction, which is hard to track with the zoomed-out camera. — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-review-heir-to-elegance/)
- Successful parries on bosses produce "flashy cinematic sequences that shave off the boss's health," some feeling "ripped straight from a battle manga." — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-review-heir-to-elegance/)
- Criticisms: combat clashes with platforming (for example, archers knocking you off platforms), and the free-flowing offensive combat is less satisfying than the defensive parry and dodge tests. — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-review-heir-to-elegance/)
- Enemies telegraph most attacks, so the player can dodge, parry or counter. "Perfect parries come with flashy cutscenes" inspired by shonen anime. In boss fights, even a missed parry can trigger a cutscene in which the player loses health. — [TechRaptor review](https://techraptor.net/gaming/reviews/prince-of-persia-lost-crown-review-its-about-time-and-space)
- Amulets change the playstyle. One example creates "a bubble of slow motion with every parry." — [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/reviews/prince-of-persia-lost-crown-review-its-about-time-and-space)

### Inferences
- The most transferable Lost Crown idea for a small game is the two-color telegraph contract: one color for "parry me" and one for "dodge me." It suits the Srivijayan fighters' existing half-second telegraph. A yellow or gold ink flare could mark parryable attacks, and a red or vermilion seal-stamp flare could mark unblockable ones.
- Parry stuns are the cheapest high-value reward: a short stun plus a free damage window. A cinematic counter can be scaled down to a 0.3 to 0.6 s slow-motion close-up or a single framed "ink splash" pose. It does not need a full cutscene.
- A meter that fills on landing hits and drains on taking damage rewards aggression and makes hits feel productive even when an enemy is not yet dead.
- Reviewers' reluctance to commit to long strings suggests a 3-hit string with a clear, short recovery. Long strings are not needed.

### Gaps
- No primary source found with frame data for The Lost Crown (hitstop length, parry window in frames, combo timings). Developer GDC talks on its combat were not located.
- IGN, Eurogamer and Game Informer reviews were not successfully fetched. Coverage here relies on GamingTrend, TechRaptor and Ubisoft's own copy.

## 2. Sands of Time trilogy, original Prince of Persia (1989), and AC Chronicles: India

### Takeaway
The 1989 original's swordplay worked as a readable profile-view duel: advance, retreat, slash, parry. It was rotoscoped from six seconds of the Errol Flynn and Basil Rathbone duel in *The Adventures of Robin Hood* and gave "blades clash high and low in a kind of balletic rhythm." Sands of Time added a two-step kill (wear an enemy down with the sword, then finish with the Dagger), plus time powers. Critics faulted its respawning enemies, camera and lack of new skills. AC Chronicles: India's combat was widely called stilted and the least interesting part, a parry and roll system bolted onto stealth.

### Cited Findings
- 1989 sword maneuvers: advance, back off, slash, parry, and a combined parry-then-slash. Enemy swordsmen show a health indicator like the Prince's. They can be killed by slashing or by being pushed into traps mid-fight. — [Wikipedia: Prince of Persia (1989)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Persia_(1989_video_game))
- The Skeleton can't be killed with the sword and must be dropped into a pit. The Shadow was created because Mechner found the game dull without combat. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Persia_(1989_video_game))
- The sword sprites were rotoscoped from the final Flynn/Rathbone duel in *The Adventures of Robin Hood*. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Persia_(1989_video_game))
- Mechner found "six seconds" of that duel in "exact profile" ("a godsend"). He spread "two-dozen snapshots" on the floor to turn it into a "repeatable pattern." The result: "the blades clash high and low in a kind of balletic rhythm." He also studied *Captain Blood*. — [Animation Obsessive, "How the Little Guy Moved"](https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/how-the-little-guy-moved)
- Mechner: "I really wanted the character to feel human." He described a "visceral sense of danger" as exciting. Losing a swordfight "wasn't particularly graphic," and a "mistimed parry" was one way death stayed close. — [Ubisoft 35th anniversary retrospective](https://news.ubisoft.com/en-us/article/6yLrRf7b0U1MBYxmlxE225/prince-of-persia-35th-anniversary-a-look-back-at-the-original-game)
- Contemporary praise: GamePro called the animation "extremely fluid" and said the controls are hard to master but effective. CGW said the game captures the feel of classic adventure films. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Persia_(1989_video_game))
- Sands of Time: sword plus Dagger of Time, one button each. Sword moves are context-sensitive to position and direction. Enemies damaged by the sword can only be killed by stabbing them with the Dagger to absorb their Sand. Wall-bounce attacks need extra inputs. Sand powers can slow time, freeze one enemy, or freeze all enemies. — [Wikipedia: Sands of Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Persia:_The_Sands_of_Time)
- SoT criticism: Edge noted difficulty spikes from respawning enemies. OXM found combat difficult at times and criticized the lack of new skills. 1UP called the camera "tricky." — [Wikipedia: Sands of Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Persia:_The_Sands_of_Time)
- AC Chronicles: India combat "is a bit stilted and relies on the same parry/rollover system as before." "When you're forced to fight is when it's the least interesting." Enemies attack from multiple directions, and combat and stealth "come a little unstuck." — [God is a Geek review](https://godisageek.com/reviews/assassins-creed-chronicles-india-review/)
- GameSpot: swordplay has "a fluidity and heft that must be accounted for before every major move," but "the response time for him to even unsheath his sword [is] not exactly quick when there's enemies all around." Stealth-takedown prompts "occasionally [fail] to appear." Praise: "every kill feels like its own victory," and well-placed enemies make room-clearing a puzzle. — [GameSpot review](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/assassins-creed-chronicles-india-review/1900-6416328/)
- India's tools: chakram (cuts ropes, stuns), smoke bombs, noisemakers, whistle, grappling hook. Helix powers give instant invisibility or one-hit kills. — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/reviews/assassins-creed-chronicles-india-review/1900-6416328/)

### Inferences
- The 1989 duel is the right model for "make it clear what my sword is doing." Fighters stand in profile at a fixed spacing, attacks come high or low, and every exchange ends in a visible clash or a clean hit. Its readability came from strict profile staging and a repeatable rhythm.
- Sands of Time's two-step "wear down, then finish" structure gives each hit a visible purpose: the enemy goes down and a finishing prompt appears. A non-gory version for Chola could be an enemy dropping to one knee or losing its weapon, then a finishing strike that knocks them out or sends them fleeing (an ink dissolve, or a dropped weapon clattering).
- AC India's lesson is negative. Sluggish weapon-draw and attack startup, plus unreliable context prompts, make combat feel stilted. Keep the startup of the player's attack fast and put the weight in contact and follow-through.

### Gaps
- No frame-level detail on 1989 parry windows or guard AI. Warrior Within's Free-Form Fighting System and Two Thrones' Speed Kill stealth system were not covered by fetched sources. Original GameSpot and IGN reviews of SoT were not fetched.
- Whether AC India's shield or heavy enemies required specific counters was not confirmed.

## 3. Game-feel techniques for melee: anticipation, contact, follow-through, hitstop, shake, reactions, sound, hit counts

### Takeaway
Hitstop is the single biggest lever. In Smash Ultimate it is `floor(damage × 0.65 + 6)` frames at 60 fps, capped at 30, so even a light hit freezes about 6 to 8 frames (100 to 130 ms). Both attacker and victim freeze. The victim shakes horizontally on the ground and vertically in the air, with the shake decaying, while the hitbox stays still. A 2024 user study found hitstop up to about 0.37 to 0.39 s rated pleasant and 0.54 s or more rated unpleasant. For the swing itself, use short anticipation, one or two smear frames along the arc, and a held follow-through. A poke or bat look usually means no arc and no follow-through.

### Cited Findings
- **Smash hitlag formula**, in frames at 60 fps. Melee: `floor(d/3 + 3)`, cap 20. Ultimate: `floor((d × 0.65 + 6) × multipliers)`, cap 30. Electric hits ×1.5. Crouch-cancel ×0.67. — [SmashWiki: Hitlag](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)
- The attacker freezes on the frame the hit lands. The victim freezes in the first frame of its flinch animation while shaking. If an attack deals no knockback, the victim gets no hitlag but the attacker still does. Purpose: a signal that the attack connected, time to plan, and emphasis that scales with damage. — [SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)
- Sakurai (Famitsu column): attacker and opponent freeze for the same time. "In general, the more damage an attack inflicts, the longer the hitstop period." There is a cap. Marth's sword tip gets increased hitstop and the edge gets reduced. Projectiles get less hitstop. Electric attacks get more. The flinch-to-hurt pose transition during hitstop takes 4 frames. — [Source Gaming translation](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/)
- Sakurai on shake: grounded characters vibrate side to side and airborne characters up and down. Amplitude starts large and shrinks over the freeze. Hurtboxes stay fixed so the shake doesn't cause misses. Intensity scales with camera distance. The attacker vibrates slightly and drifts very slightly during the freeze. — [Source Gaming](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/); the same eight techniques are summarized from his YouTube video at [Nintendo Wire](https://nintendowire.com/news/2022/12/12/this-week-in-sakurai-12-5-12-11-fine-tuning-hit-stop-and-cheating-the-system/)
- Sakurai notes that hitstop is most prominent in 2D fighters, and that 3D fighters and *Dynasty Warriors* use little of it. — [Source Gaming](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/)
- **Empirical hitstop study** (Tomizawa & Ishikawa, GRAPP 2024). They tested 0.0 to 0.7 s across sword, bow and mixed scenes. The mean "pleasant" duration was 0.37 s (sword scene) and 0.39 s (mixed), and the mean "unpleasant" duration was 0.56 to 0.57 s. Pleasant/unpleasant boundaries were 0.537 s and 0.55 s. Longer hitstop tended to be more uncomfortable. Dynamic, varying hitstop was rated more enjoyable than fixed. — [SciTePress paper](https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2024/124614/124614.pdf)
- Vlambeer "Art of Screenshake" effect list, as recreated: lower time-to-kill, bigger and faster projectiles, muzzle flash, impact effect, hit reaction, enemy knockback, permanence (corpses or debris stay), camera lerp, screenshake, player knockback, hit pause, weapon recoil. No numbers were given in text. — [DK Liao recreation devlog](https://dkliao.itch.io/the-art-of-screenshake-recreation/devlog/451576/quick-breakdown-of-all-the-effects)
- **Swing animation** (Raymond Schlitter, pixel artist). Too much anticipation feels laggy and too much recovery feels stuck, so keep anticipation short. "A single smear frame is often enough," following a natural path from stance to follow-through. Hold the follow-through a little longer than other frames. Freeze briefly on the impact frame of strong hits. Add overshoot on recovery. Slightly elongate the weapon at full extension. The sample sword covers a 90° arc from overhead to straight ahead. Wide arcs read as slow and strong, narrow arcs as fast and weak. — [Slynyrd Pixelblog 9: Melee Attacks](https://slynyrd.com/blog/2018/9/8/pixelblog-9-melee-attacks)
- Dead Cells: screen shake when heavy weapons connect, "a satisfying swoosh" per swing and a noticeable impact per hit, distinct animation per weapon. Rolling behind an enemy lets you resume a combo. — [Entertainium](https://entertainium.co/2018/10/19/dead-cells-is-great-because-it-feels-great/)
- Hollow Knight: hitting enemies or walls with the nail pushes the player back slightly (recoil). Hitting downward on a target bounces the player up (pogo). Recoil makes actions feel heavier. No numbers given. — [WPI DGMI wiki: Recoil](https://dgmiwiki.wpi.edu/mechanic/recoil)

### Inferences
- **Why it looks like a cricket bat** (diagnosis, not sourced): a bat swing reads as a flat horizontal sweep pivoting from the shoulders, with no wrist turn and no edge alignment. A sword slash reads as a curved arc where the blade edge leads and the tip travels farthest, ending in a follow-through past the target, often diagonal (high-to-low or low-to-high). The fixes follow from Schlitter: a diagonal arc of about 90 to 150°, one crescent smear frame (an ink-brush stroke fits this game's style), a held follow-through pose, and slight blade elongation at full extension.
- **Suggested timings at 60 fps** (unsourced, practitioner convention consistent with the cited ranges):
  - Light-slash anticipation: 3 to 6 frames (50 to 100 ms).
  - Active frames: 2 to 4.
  - Follow-through and recovery: 8 to 15 frames.
  - Hitstop: about 4 to 6 frames on string hits 1 and 2, 8 to 12 on the finisher, 12 to 20 on a parry or posture break. That stays well under the study's ~0.37 s pleasant mean.
  - Vary hitstop per hit, which the study found more enjoyable.
- **Screen shake** (unsourced convention): small, 2 to 4 px for about 100 to 150 ms on finishers, parries and guard breaks only. Apply it on hit, not on every swing, and decay it, as Sakurai does for character shake.
- **Enemy reactions**: freeze the victim in its flinch pose for the hitstop and shake it horizontally (Sakurai). Then play a short knockback: small slides on normal hits and a larger push or launch on the finisher. Brief player recoil (Hollow Knight) also helps when hitting a shield.
- **Hit count** (unsourced): basic fodder should die on the 3-hit string or its finisher, or within 2 strings. More than that makes the "bunting" feeling worse, because each hit seems to do nothing.
- **Non-gory feedback for an ink-and-watercolor style**: ink-splash impact bursts in a non-blood color (indigo or black ink, gold flecks), cloth tears and sash flutter, sparks and a bright flash on metal (blade-on-shield, blade-on-blade clashes), dust puffs, a white-tinted flash on the victim sprite for 1 to 2 frames, dropped weapons that stay on the floor ("permanence"), and paper-cut or brushstroke dissolve on defeat. Layer audio: a whoosh on swing, then a distinct thud or cloth sound on a flesh hit versus a ringing "clang" on parry or shield, as in the Sekiro "steel against steel" cue.

### Gaps
- No primary-source frame numbers were found for Hades, Hollow Knight, Dead Cells or Celeste hitstop or shake. GDC talk transcripts (Jan Willem Nijman, Supergiant, Team Cherry, Motion Twin) were not retrieved. The Vlambeer talk's exact values were not in text form.
- No source on numeric knockback distances.

## 4. Enemy design for a small roster: telegraphs, punish windows, parry rewards, shield-bearers

### Takeaway
Make every enemy action telegraphed, with exactly one learnable counter (Sekiro's "hard counters"). Reward parries with stun plus damage or posture break. Make passive play costly by letting enemy posture regenerate. A shield-bearer is interesting when it is a puzzle with several solutions: guard break, getting behind it, parry-into-opening, or a launcher. It is tedious when it is a damage sponge that absorbs the same attack repeatedly. That design conclusion is inferred, not sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Sekiro: "All enemy moves are telegraphed; to every action there's a specific counter that you need to perform." "It's a game of hard counters." — [SuperJump: The Art and Science of Sekiro's Combat](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/the-art-and-science-of-sekiros-combat/)
- Sekiro posture: deflects and hits drain posture, and a broken posture leaves the enemy "off-balance" for a Deathblow. "A missed deflect seamlessly turns into a guard," so mistimed parries aren't total failures. Enemies regain posture over time, so "if all you do is wait for them to act you'll give them time to recover their posture." — [SuperJump](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/the-art-and-science-of-sekiros-combat/)
- Lost Crown: parrying a basic attack briefly stuns. Parrying a yellow-flagged attack triggers a big cinematic counter. Red attacks must be dodged. — [Ubisoft Montpellier](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/montpellier/news/NvEbHCYiO8TjMNmfchQ6X/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-will-put-your-combat-platforming-and-puzzlesolving-skills-to-the-test)
- Lost Crown: some enemies reward specific moves that leave them defenseless, but this breaks down in multi-enemy fights. — [GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-review-heir-to-elegance/)
- 1989 PoP gave enemies two ways to die: deplete health, or push them into traps mid-duel. Some enemies (the Skeleton) are immune to the sword and must be solved with the environment. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Persia_(1989_video_game))
- AC India: being attacked from multiple directions at once with a parry/roll system led to frequent desyncs and "messy" fights. — [God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/reviews/assassins-creed-chronicles-india-review/)
- Smash: on a perfect shield, the attacker gets hitlag and the defender gets none. Rewarding the defender with frame advantage this way is a concrete parry-reward mechanism. — [SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag)

### Inferences
- **Srivijayan fighter** (existing half-second telegraph): keep it at about 0.5 s and make it two-tone (yellow means parryable, red means unblockable thrust). A successful parry should give a stun of about 0.6 to 1 s with a clear "reeling" pose, enough for one full 3-hit string. A missed parry could degrade into a block with chip posture, as in Sekiro, so parrying isn't all-or-nothing.
- **Shield-bearer, design to avoid tedium** (unsourced):
  - Front hits are blocked with a visible clang, sparks and player recoil, so the player instantly knows why no damage happened.
  - Each blocked hit visibly cracks or dents a shield-guard meter or the shield sprite. 3 to 4 blocked hits produce a guard break (shield knocked aside, a 1 to 1.5 s opening).
  - Alternatives: roll or vault behind it (a PoP vault move fits), parry its shield-bash for an instant open, or knock it into a hazard (as in 1989).
  - Its own attack is a slow telegraphed bash or lunge that drops the guard, which is the punish window.
- **Sentinels**: give them a single clear vulnerable state (for example after an attack or when turned away) and make stealth or back attacks a one-hit takedown, as AC India does. Make sure the takedown prompt is reliable, unlike India's.
- **Mixing**: limit how many enemies can attack at once (token or attack-slot system, unsourced convention) to avoid the AC India "attacked from all sides" mess and the Lost Crown multi-enemy breakdown.

### Gaps
- No primary source found specifically on shield-enemy design (for example a GDC talk or a Hollow Knight or Dead Cells shielder analysis). The shield-bearer recommendations are inference.
- No sourced numbers for parry windows (Sekiro, Lost Crown).

## 5. How to show an enemy's state so the player knows what the sword is doing

### Takeaway
Readability comes from three layers. First, every hit produces a distinct, immediate reaction (freeze, shake, flinch pose, knockback) that differs for "damaged," "blocked" and "parried." Second, a persistent state display (health and/or posture or guard bar, or diegetic sprite changes). Third, telegraphs with a consistent color code. Sources support the hitstop and reaction layer, the health indicator (PoP 1989), and color telegraphs (Lost Crown). Specific UI layouts are inference.

### Cited Findings
- Hitlag "acts as a visual signal that an attack connected." The victim freezes in its flinch frame while shaking, and hitstop scales with damage, so stronger hits read as stronger. — [SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Hitlag); [Source Gaming](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/)
- Sakurai blends in-between frames into the victim's damage pose (a 4-frame flinch-to-hurt transition during the freeze). — [Source Gaming](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/); [Nintendo Wire](https://nintendowire.com/news/2022/12/12/this-week-in-sakurai-12-5-12-11-fine-tuning-hit-stop-and-cheating-the-system/)
- 1989 PoP gave enemy swordsmen a health indicator like the player's, making duel progress legible. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Persia_(1989_video_game))
- Lost Crown uses glowing eye color (yellow or red) on the enemy itself as a diegetic state and telegraph signal. — [Ubisoft Montpellier](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/studio/montpellier/news/NvEbHCYiO8TjMNmfchQ6X/prince-of-persia-the-lost-crown-will-put-your-combat-platforming-and-puzzlesolving-skills-to-the-test)
- Sekiro's posture bar makes progress visible even when health barely moves, and the broken state is shown as "off-balance," ready for a Deathblow. — [SuperJump](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/the-art-and-science-of-sekiros-combat/)
- Vlambeer's list includes "hit reaction," "enemy knockback" and "permanence" as separate feedback channels. — [DK Liao devlog](https://dkliao.itch.io/the-art-of-screenshake-recreation/devlog/451576/quick-breakdown-of-all-the-effects)

### Inferences
- Give "what did my sword just do?" three distinct, instantly different answers:
  - **Hit**: victim freezes and white-flashes, shakes horizontally, plays its flinch pose, then slides back. An indigo ink-burst plays and a cloth sound or thud sounds.
  - **Blocked**: no flash on the victim, a metal spark and clang at the contact point, the player recoils backward, the shield guard meter chips.
  - **Parried**: a long freeze of about 12 to 20 frames, a gold flash ring, a reeling-enemy pose, and a short slow-motion or zoom.
- Add a small segmented bar over each damaged enemy, showing health and, for the shield-bearer, a separate guard bar. This beats a floating number for a painted style. Segments matching the hit count (for example 3 pips for fodder) let the player predict kills.
- Diegetic progression fits the art style: the enemy turban or sash tears, a weapon is dropped, posture visibly sags, and an ink "crack" appears on the shield.
- Facing and contact point matter: spawn the impact effect at the actual blade-to-body intersection, angled along the slash direction. A slash-direction-aligned ink streak is what separates "slash" from "bat."

### Gaps
- No source fetched on HUD and bar conventions in 2D action games (for example Hollow Knight's lack of enemy health bars versus Dead Cells' bars) or on player-testing results for readability.
