# Chola: Tides of Bronze — Vision

Owner: creative-director. Readers: everyone. Fourth draft, 2026-10-25,
revised after Gaurav's rulings in `docs/DECISIONS.md` (through 13), two
vision-critic reviews in `docs/reviews/`, and `docs/RESEARCH.md`.

Budget: 1,200 words for sections 1 to 8 (sections 1 to 7 at most 900,
section 8 under 300), counting prose and table cells, not headings. Revision
notes sit outside the budget.

## 1. The game in one sentence

A common Chola soldier of the fleet runs from Thanjavur's temple to the
Srivijaya shore, crossing between the depth layers of an ink-and-watercolor
world at lamplit gates.

## 2. Player fantasy

You are nobody: one soldier on one ship among many, and the emperor will
never learn your name. A sword at your hip, the sea ahead, and your
century's largest event at eye level.

Step through a lamplit gate and you walk into the painting's haze.
LittleBigPlanet and Mutant Mudds also change layers; neither paints them or
makes you earn the crossing.

## 3. Pillars

### Pillar 1: The painting has depth, and only Chola is red

Every scene is one ink-and-watercolor painting in its own place's colors,
with three depths to stand in, crossed only where marked; crimson is the
only true red and means Chola.

Approves: each place's palette (DECISIONS.md, 17); the gate leap (16); a
marked rope or vine (15).

Rejects: any red that reads as crimson outside Chola (an awning, a
Srivijayan, a telegraph; 23, 24); flat, low-contrast layers.

### Pillar 2: One of the crew

The player is an ordinary person of the fleet, who sees rulers, battles,
and treaties from the deck and the road and never decides them.

Approves: a fight the road forces, first in Kadaram; a parry (24); buying what a sailor needs (26); interstitials
told from the road.

Rejects: a fight staged for its own sake (an arena, a wave, an enemy where
no route passes); a Chola or named opponent; any reward for a kill; buying
rank, men, or ships.

### Pillar 3: The voyage only goes east

Every level is a leg of one voyage east. What the player finds may be spent
or remembered, but the game never scores, grades, or tallies what he missed.

Approves (DECISIONS.md, 26): kasu that lead the eye; kasu spent at ports on a
pilot or an offering; detours marked on the voyage map; rare named
kasu that open history cards. Map and cards show what was found, never
empty slots.

Rejects: score, grades, lives, quotas, an end-of-level tally of misses;
grinding in one place to earn more.

## 4. Audience

Settled (DECISIONS.md, 2): adults first, finishable by a ten-year-old.

Core [assumed]: adults 25 to 50, diaspora or Ponniyin Selvan readers, with a
laptop; ten playtests will say how many play games. Outer ring [assumed]:
their children, 10 and up, who play Hollow Knight and will tolerate history
if the jumping is good.

What makes both stop: a cheap death, a fall that resets a whole stretch or a
guard who hits from off screen.

## 5. Tone and references

Tone: quiet, painted, unhurried. References: DECISIONS.md 13, 14.

**Donkey Kong Country (Rare) and The Jungle Book (SNES).** Each level built
around a traversal piece native to its place, taught where a fall is safe,
then combined; vine swings; blocks pushed to open the way; secrets marked
on the map. Not DKC's lives, letters, or completion percentage (Pillar 3).

**Prince of Persia (1989; The Sands of Time onward; The Lost Crown).** Moves
that chain, wall-jump to beam to ledge, so the architecture is the puzzle
and a route reads before it is run; the parry; gentle checkpoints.
Not The Lost Crown's ability gates and backtracking (Pillar 3).

**Assassin's Creed Chronicles: India (Climax).** Our closest neighbor: 2.5D
stealth in painted South Asia, with depth planes. We take its legible
patrols: route and reach read before the player commits. We differ: a
guard who sees the player chases him but catches him only if he stops in
reach (DECISIONS.md, 8), and no Chola is fought; no takedowns from hiding;
no score or ranks; only Chola wears red; a crewman, never an assassin.

## 6. Historical stance

Three rules. First, anything shown as fact carries the historian's
[documented] tag or is recast as Ilamaran's belief, the end-screen claim
first (RESEARCH.md 3.3). Second, invention is free below the
rank of commander, in spoken lines, and in layouts; places, dates, rulers,
dress, weapons, coins, and architecture follow `docs/RESEARCH.md`. Third,
Srivijayans are a kingdom with a navy and temples, never faceless. The raid
is one campaign, after Kulke; the sources screen notes the dispute.

Settled (DECISIONS.md, 3 and 4): Ilamaran never harms civilians or kills
Chola people; the ending adds one line of cost. Falls, not gore.

## 7. Scope for the first release

Three levels of 3 to 4 minutes, about 10 to 12 in all (DECISIONS.md, 12).
Each adds one or two native traversal pieces (11, 14); the sheathed sword is
legible from the start (10). Browser, keyboard and touch. Levels 1 and 2 are
traversal and stealth, the Chola guard an obstacle absent from level 3 (3).
One painted Srivijayan enemy, slots 17 and 18; gate and mid-band slots
filled; characters animated by cut-out rigs from one painted image each
(19). Three interstitials, one ending, one sources screen.

Out of scope: saving, difficulty settings, voice, new music, Tamil
localization, boss fights, upgrades, leaderboards, app stores, and the
ship-deck level (release two, DECISIONS.md, 1).

## 8. Open questions for Gaurav

Earlier questions are ruled in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

**1. Do the voyage map and history cards survive closing the browser?**
Ruling 26 makes them worth keeping, but section 7 puts saving out of scope.
Options: (a) they last one sitting only; (b) the browser remembers them on
that device, with nothing sent anywhere; (c) full save and resume.
Recommendation: (b). It is cheap, a found card should stay found, and full
save can wait.
