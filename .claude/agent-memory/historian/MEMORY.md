# Historian agent memory

## Verified sources and what each is good for

Full text, free, reachable from this environment:
- **SII II (Hultzsch), Thanjavur inscriptions**: whatisindia.com hosts the
  English translations with Hultzsch's introduction. Read nos. 1 (kalasam,
  para. 18, 25th year/275th day), 57 n. 2 (tirumeykappar gate guards), 63–66
  (no. 65: 48 pidarar + 2 drummers; no. 66: 400 temple women + ~110 men,
  dated 29th year c. 1014, not 1011). Introduction lists 31 regiments.
- **Balasubrahmanyam, Middle Chola Temples (1975), Thanjavur chapter**:
  wisdomlib.org transcription. Figures: base 30.18 m square, 13 talas,
  height 63.41 m, capstone 7.77 m square ~80 tons, stupi "said to be" 3.81 m
  but measured 3.35 m. Gives no enclosure dimensions.
- **Song Shi juan 489** (zh.wikisource.org): Zhunian entries 1015 (52
  people; 21,100 liang pearls, 60 tusks, 60 jin frankincense) and 1033 (105
  liang pearls, 100 tusks); Sanfoqi embassy 1028.
- **Kanakasabai, Tamils 1800 Years Ago** (1956 reprint, archive.org):
  pp. 130–131 ox-hide bucklers, lances, generals in coats of mail,
  breast-plates in arsenals; cites Silappadikaram XV. Colonial-era; use as
  pointer to poems only.
- **UNESCO nomination file, Great Living Chola Temples (2004)**: enclosure
  c. 240 x 120 m; "65 m" tower.
- **ISEAS bookshop page for Kulke/Kesavapany/Sakhuja 2009**: full table of
  contents (ch. 5 Subbarayalu "A Note on the Navy"; ch. 4 Sakhuja nautical).
- **coins.lakdiva.org**: Rajaraja kahavanu 4.30 g, 20.2 mm, fineness 0.47.
  Design (standing king with lamp / seated figure with conch, "Sri Raja
  Raja") from a Spink sale record citing Codrington 104, Mitchiner 318.
- scroll.in Chudamani vihara piece (Mahalingam 1948, Fontein 1980);
  museumvolunteersjmm.com on Bujang Valley (Sungai Mas, Pengkalan Bujang).
- Wikipedia "Tamil copper-plate inscriptions": Chennai Museum Chola seal,
  seated tiger + two fish + bow.

Blocked or abstract-only: halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr (Manguin) blocks
fetches; Cambridge Core and academia.edu give abstracts only; Sastri,
Spencer, Michell, Elgood not opened (Elgood 2015 p. 83 known only via the
Ethnographic Arms & Armour forum quote).

## Corrections received (fact-checker, 2026-10-25)

- **Sword**: "straight only" was wrong. Elgood 2015 p. 83: forward-curved
  South Indian blade attested from 7th c. (Mamallapuram gana), continues
  for centuries; late form sosun pattah. Only back-curved talwar (post-1565)
  is out of period. Advice now: straight broad OR forward-curved; never talwar.
